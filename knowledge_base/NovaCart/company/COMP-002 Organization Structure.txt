This document defines the formal organizational framework, executive leadership hierarchy, operational departmental boundaries, cross-functional reporting structures, and governance ownership rules for NovaCart Technologies Pvt. Ltd. (hereinafter referred to as "NovaCart").
As a primary core document (COMP-002) in the NovaCart Enterprise Knowledge Base (NEKB), this policy establishes clear authority domains, operational handoffs, policy ownership assignments, and collaborative interaction protocols across all organizational divisions in all operating regions, including India, Singapore, and the United Arab Emirates (UAE).

Policy Overview
NovaCart enforces a matrix organizational architecture designed to balance global functional scale with regional execution agility. Operational departments operate under centralized policy governance managed from global headquarters in Bangalore, India, while maintaining regional execution nodes in Singapore and Dubai.
Core Governance Principles
1. Unambiguous Ownership: Every business domain, policy document, and operational process must have exactly one single Accountable department owner.
2. Functional Escalation Clarity: Reporting lines and operational handoffs must follow explicit, predefined paths to prevent administrative friction or delayed customer resolutions.
3. Cross-Functional Integration: Departmental siloing is strictly prohibited; operational processes spanning multiple domains must operate under joint SLA commitments.
4. Policy Governance Alignment: All departmental SOPs and internal guidelines must align with COMP-001 and COMP-002.

1. Document Overview & Purpose
This document defines the organizational hierarchy, executive leadership roles, departmental responsibilities, reporting lines, operational interaction models, and ownership boundaries within NovaCart Technologies Pvt. Ltd. (hereafter referred to as "NovaCart").
As NovaCart expands its hyper-local e-commerce, automated fulfillment network, and proprietary logistics platform, establishing clear structural boundaries and governance models is critical. This document serves as the single source of truth for:
* Defining executive and departmental authority.
* Mapping cross-functional workflows and dependencies across engineering, logistics, warehouse operations, returns management, finance, customer support, human resources, security, product management, sales, and legal affairs.
* Assigning formal ownership of current and future corporate policies, operating procedures, and compliance guidelines.
* Resolving inter-departmental conflicts through established escalation matrices and business rules.
All provisions within COMP-002 align with the foundational values, corporate goals, and operational principles established in COMP-001.
2. Executive Leadership & Governance
NovaCart operates under a matrix-aligned functional organizational hierarchy designed to maintain rapid execution at local fulfillment nodes while ensuring centralized governance over platform engineering, risk, compliance, and financial management.
2.1 Board of Directors
The Board of Directors represents shareholders and provides fiduciary oversight, strategic direction, and corporate governance. The Board approves long-term capital expenditure, executive compensation, strategic M&A activities, and corporate restructuring.
2.2 Chief Executive Officer (CEO)
The Chief Executive Officer holds primary executive responsibility for overall company strategy, market expansion, organizational alignment, and financial performance.
* Direct Reports: Chief Operating Officer (COO), Chief Technology Officer (CTO), Chief Financial Officer (CFO), Chief Revenue Officer (CRO), Chief Legal Officer (CLO), Head of Human Resources (CHRO).
* Primary Mandate: Executive leadership, board alignment, corporate positioning, and strategic growth.
2.3 Chief Operating Officer (COO)
The Chief Operating Officer manages day-to-day business operations, fulfillment center networks, supply chain infrastructure, customer experience, and physical security operations.
* Direct Reports: VP of Supply Chain & Operations, Head of Logistics, Head of Warehouse & Fulfillment, Head of Returns Management, Head of Customer Support, Head of Corporate Security & Loss Prevention.
* Primary Mandate: Operational efficiency, last-mile speed, fulfillment accuracy, inventory integrity, and regional facility safety.
2.4 Chief Technology Officer (CTO)
The Chief Technology Officer leads software development, platform architecture, infrastructure engineering, data engineering, product management, and cybersecurity.
* Direct Reports: VP of Engineering, Head of Product Management, Head of Information Security & Data Privacy, Head of IT Infrastructure.
* Primary Mandate: Platform reliability, software innovation, scalability, cloud infrastructure cost-efficiency, and system security.
2.5 Chief Financial Officer (CFO)
The Chief Financial Officer oversees corporate financial planning, accounting, treasury, tax compliance, vendor payouts, and fraud prevention finance systems.
* Direct Reports: Head of Finance & Accounting, Head of Financial Planning & Analysis (FP&A), Head of Risk & Internal Audit.
* Primary Mandate: Financial compliance, capital allocation, unit economics optimization, and fiscal reporting.
2.6 Chief Revenue Officer (CRO)
The Chief Revenue Officer leads revenue-generating functions, seller acquisition, merchant relations, digital marketing, and enterprise sales.
* Direct Reports: Head of Seller Acquisition, Head of Enterprise Partnerships, Head of Digital Marketing & Brand Strategy.
* Primary Mandate: Gross Merchandise Value (GMV) growth, merchant onboarding, advertising revenue, and commercial monetization.
2.7 Chief Legal Officer (CLO)
The Chief Legal Officer directs legal affairs, regulatory compliance, platform terms of service, consumer dispute policies, and intellectual property.
* Direct Reports: Head of Corporate Legal, Head of Regulatory & Policy Compliance.
* Primary Mandate: Regulatory safety, litigation management, contract oversight, and compliance with national and regional commerce laws.
3. Departmental Roles, Responsibilities, and Ownership Boundaries
NovaCart consists of twelve primary operational departments. Each department maintains clear functional boundaries, system access privileges, and policy ownership mandates.
3.1 Customer Support (CS)
* Core Responsibilities:
   * Operating 24/7 omni-channel customer service (chat, voice, email, and ticketing).
   * Resolving buyer inquiries regarding order status, delivery delays, missing items, and promotional codes.
   * Executing immediate customer compensations (refund credits, wallet top-ups) within defined authority thresholds.
   * Escalating recurring platform issues or systemic logistics failures to Engineering and Logistics.
* Key Performance Indicators (KPIs): First Contact Resolution (FCR), Average Handling Time (AHT), Net Promoter Score (NPS), Customer Satisfaction Score (CSAT).
* Policy Ownership: Customer Dispute Resolution Policy, Customer Compensation Framework.
3.2 Engineering (ENG)
* Core Responsibilities:
   * Developing, deploying, and maintaining NovaCart’s web platforms, mobile applications, microservices, and databases.
   * Building internal tooling for warehouse management systems (WMS), route optimization engines, and seller management portals.
   * Maintaining 99.99% system uptime, executing continuous integration/continuous deployment (CI/CD) pipelines, and conducting load testing.
   * Patching technical vulnerabilities, optimizing database queries, and managing cloud infrastructure cost structures.
* Key Performance Indicators (KPIs): Platform Uptime, Mean Time to Detect/Resolve (MTTD/MTTR), API Response Time, Sprint Completion Rate.
* Policy Ownership: Software Development Lifecycle (SDLC) Policy, Change Management Policy, Technical Standard Operating Procedures.
3.3 Finance & Accounting (FIN)
* Core Responsibilities:
   * Managing general ledger, accounts payable, accounts receivable, payroll execution, and corporate tax filings.
   * Reconciling gateway transaction logs with internal order ledgers to ensure zero financial drift.
   * Executing weekly seller payouts, logistics provider disbursements, and vendor invoicing.
   * Conducting budget variance analysis and maintaining financial forecasting models.
* Key Performance Indicators (KPIs): Financial Reconciliation Variance (<0.01%), Days Sales Outstanding (DSO), Month-End Close Cycle Time.
* Policy Ownership: Corporate Financial Authority Matrix, Travel & Expense Policy, Seller Payout Policy, Revenue Recognition Policy.
3.4 Operations & Planning (OPS)
* Core Responsibilities:
   * Planning network-wide operational capacity across cities, fulfillment zones, and hub clusters.
   * Managing demand forecasting models to align labor hours with anticipated daily volume spikes.
   * Monitoring middle-mile logistics routes and inter-facility inventory movements.
   * Defining regional operational SLAs for last-mile delivery teams.
* Key Performance Indicators (KPIs): Order Fulfillment Cost per Unit, Capacity Utilization Rate, Order Processing Cycle Time.
* Policy Ownership: Standard Operating Procedures for Network Capacity Planning, Incident Command Framework for Operational Disruptions.
3.5 Logistics & Fleet Management (LOG)
* Core Responsibilities:
   * Managing last-mile delivery fleets, rider dispatching systems, and third-party logistics (3PL) partner integrations.
   * Optimizing dynamic dispatching engines, driver routing algorithms, and delivery agent payout structures.
   * Overseeing regional delivery hubs, rider onboarding centers, and vehicle maintenance programs.
   * Tracking fuel efficiency, route compliance, delivery failure rates, and rider safety metrics.
* Key Performance Indicators (KPIs): On-Time Delivery Rate (OTD), Delivery Cost per Order, Rider Retention Rate, Order Delivery Failure Rate.
* Policy Ownership: Last-Mile Fleet Operations Standard, Delivery Partner Conduct Policy, Fleet Safety Framework.
3.6 Warehouse & Fulfillment (WH)

* Key Performance Indicators (KPIs): Pick-and-Pack Error Rate, Inbound Putaway Time, Cycle Count Accuracy, Inventory Shrinkage Rate.
* Policy Ownership: Warehouse Storage Safety Standard, Inventory Handling & Staging Policy, Fulfillment Operating Guide.
3.7 Returns & Reverse Logistics (RET)
* Core Responsibilities:
   * Managing reverse logistics pick-ups from customer locations back to processing hubs.

   * Initiating automated order restock requests and triggering real-time customer refund workflows in conjunction with Finance.
* Key Performance Indicators (KPIs): Return Processing Turnaround Time, Inspection Accuracy Rate, Reverse Logistics Cost per Unit.
* Policy Ownership: Return Inspection Standard Operating Procedure, Liquidation & Refurbishment Policy, Customer Return Guidelines.
3.8 Information Security & Physical Security (SEC)
* Core Responsibilities:
   * Protecting physical assets, distribution centers, corporate offices, and cloud architecture from internal and external threats.
   * Enforcing role-based access control (RBAC), multi-factor authentication (MFA), and zero-trust network policies.
   * Investigating inventory loss, rider fraud, organized retail crime, and internal financial theft.
   * Conducting vulnerability assessments, managing intrusion detection systems, and coordinating physical security personnel.
* Key Performance Indicators (KPIs): Time to Mitigate Vulnerabilities, Loss Prevention Incident Resolution Rate, Security Audit Pass Rate.
* Policy Ownership: Enterprise Physical Security Policy, Information Security & Data Protection Policy, Incident Response Plan.
3.9 Product Management (PM)
* Core Responsibilities:

* Key Performance Indicators (KPIs): Feature Adoption Rate, Delivery Against Product Roadmap, User Friction Index.
* Policy Ownership: Product Development Lifecycle Framework, Feature Prioritization Standard.
3.10 Sales & Merchant Relations (SAL)

	5. Cross-Departmental Operational Workflows
Departments must not operate as isolated silos. Operational success at NovaCart requires continuous integration between software systems, physical logistics, financial reconciliation, and customer service. Below are the mandated sequential workflows for core business activities.
5.1 Order-to-Delivery Standard Workflow
This workflow defines the operational handoffs required to fulfill a customer order from initial platform check-out to final door-step delivery.
1. Order Placement (Product / Engineering): The customer places an order via the application; the payment gateway validates funds, and the transaction is written to the core database.
2. Order Routing (Engineering / Warehouse): The order engine routes the manifest to the geographically optimal dark store based on real-time inventory staging data.
3. Pick and Pack (Warehouse): Warehouse staff receive the pick-list on handheld terminals, locate items, conduct scan verification, pack items into tamper-evident bags, and update the order state to "Staged."
4. Dispatch Assignment (Logistics / Engineering): The automated dispatch engine assigns a nearby last-mile delivery rider based on route optimization parameters.
5. Rider Pickup (Warehouse / Logistics): The rider scans the package barcode at the dark store staging dock, transferring custody from Warehouse to Logistics.
6. Last-Mile Delivery (Logistics): The rider navigates to the customer location, collects digital proof of delivery (OTP verification or photo), and marks the order "Delivered."
7. Financial Settlement (Finance): The ledger reconciles order payment against merchant inventory liability and logistics delivery fee allocations.
5.2 Customer Return & Refund Workflow
This workflow defines the process when a customer requests a return or refund for an order.
1. Request Initiation (Customer Support / Product): The customer submits a return request through the mobile application, selecting reason codes and uploading required evidence (e.g., photos of damaged goods).
2. Rule-Based Pre-Approval (Customer Support / Operations): The platform auto-approves qualifying returns based on business rules or routes edge-cases to a Customer Support agent.
3. Reverse Logistics Pickup (Logistics): A delivery agent is routed to pick up the item from the customer, issue a digital pickup receipt, and transport the package to the returns hub.
4. Item Inspection (Returns Management): Returns hub inspectors perform physical verification to confirm product identity, serial number alignment, and condition.
5. Inventory Dispositioning (Returns / Warehouse): The item is flagged as "Restockable" (returned to active inventory), "RTV" (returned to vendor), or "Salvage" (liquidation).
6. Financial Trigger (Finance / Customer Support): The inspection approval automatically triggers the financial ledger engine to process a credit balance refund or original payment source reversal.
5.3 Merchant Onboarding & Catalog Ingestion Workflow
This workflow governs the addition of new commercial partners and product listings to the platform.
1. Commercial Terms Negotiation (Sales): The Sales team secures agreements with a new merchant regarding commission rates, inventory commitments, and service level obligations.
2. Legal & Compliance Review (Legal / Finance): Legal verifies business licenses, tax identity numbers, and contract execution; Finance sets up merchant payout profiles.
3. Catalog Upload & Taxonomy Mapping (Product / Sales): The merchant's Product Stock Keeping Units (SKUs) are uploaded, mapped to standard catalog categories, and verified for pricing compliance.
4. Inbound Warehouse Receiving (Warehouse / Operations): The merchant ships initial inventory stock to assigned distribution hubs for receiving verification and barcode tagging.
5. Listing Activation (Product / Engineering): Upon physical inbound verification, inventory counts publish live to the buyer platform.
6. Business Rules Governing Departmental Ownership
To prevent operational ambiguity, preserve accountability, and eliminate ownership overlaps, NovaCart enforces the following structural business rules:
1. Single-Department Policy Ownership Rule: Every corporate policy, procedure, or guideline must have exactly one primary owning department. Secondary departments may act as reviewers, but final accountability rests solely with the primary head of department.
2. Data Record Custody Boundary:
   * Customer Master Data: Owned exclusively by Security & Legal (Data Privacy). Access is governed by RBAC.
   * Financial & Transaction Ledgers: Owned exclusively by Finance. Engineering holds maintenance responsibility for underlying database architecture, but Finance governs data alteration rules.
   * Physical Inventory Ledger: Owned by Warehouse until handoff to Logistics. Once scanned for dispatch, custody transfers to Logistics.
3. Financial Authorization Limit Rules: No department head has unilateral authority to commit corporate funds without CFO co-signoff if the expense exceeds $25,000 USD (or local currency equivalent). All custom software vendor tools must receive CTO approval prior to contract signing by Legal.
4. Platform Change Control Mandate: Engineering may not deploy updates that modify customer refund policies, merchant payouts, or rider compensation structures without written approval from Customer Support, Finance, and Logistics respectively.
5. Dispute Resolution Rule: In the event of an operational disagreement between departments regarding order failure attribution (e.g., Warehouse claiming bad packaging vs. Logistics claiming rough transport):
   * Initial review is conducted jointly by Head of Warehouse and Head of Logistics.
   * If unresolved within 24 hours, the issue escalates to the Chief Operating Officer, whose decision is binding.
6. Security Override Rule: The Security department (Information and Physical) possesses absolute authority to lock down compromised systems, halt facility access, or suspend employee accounts during an active security incident without prior authorization from business unit managers.
7. Examples of Cross-Department Collaboration
Cross-departmental execution is demonstrated in standard real-world operational scenarios encountered within the NovaCart ecosystem.
7.1 Scenario A: Automated Fraud Detection & Interception
* Trigger: An automated security algorithm flags an unusually high volume of high-value electronics purchases using mismatched credit card geographical locations.
* Collaboration Sequence:
   1. Security identifies the threat vector and tags the flag as high probability fraud.
   2. Engineering updates platform fraud rules immediately to freeze outgoing orders tied to the suspicious accounts.
   3. Warehouse receives an automated hold alert on physical packages currently on staging racks, preventing rider handoff.
   4. Finance halts payment processing and reverses unauthorized charges before bank settlement.
   5. Customer Support receives flagged communication scripts if affected account holders contact help lines.
7.2 Scenario B: Peak Season Promotional Surge
* Trigger: Sales plans a major regional shopping festival expected to generate a 300% surge in order volume over 72 hours.
* Collaboration Sequence:
   1. Sales shares sales volume forecasts by category with Operations 30 days in advance.
   2. Operations & Warehouse adjust dark store staffing schedules, add temporary shift capacity, and secure additional packing materials.
   3. Logistics recruits third-party delivery contractors and adjusts dynamic fleet dispatch thresholds.
   4. Engineering provisions extra cloud compute instances, conducts stress testing, and enforces a platform code-freeze during event hours.
   5. Finance approves temporary overtime budgets and adjusts payout liquidity lines to handle high-frequency merchant settlements.
7.3 Scenario C: Product Quality Defect Recall
* Trigger: Multiple customers report through Customer Support that a recently shipped batch of fresh dairy products has passed expiry dates or suffered cold-chain failure.
* Collaboration Sequence:
   1. Customer Support flags systemic ticket clusters in the customer incident portal.
   2. Operations & Warehouse execute immediate quarantine procedures for the specific batch SKU across all regional fulfillment nodes.
   3. Logistics halts in-flight deliveries for orders containing the affected batch.
   4. Legal & Sales contact the merchant vendor to issue a formal non-compliance notice and initiate inventory return-to-vendor (RTV) protocols.
   5. Customer Support & Finance push proactive automated app notifications, issuing full refunds to impacted buyers.

	9. Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)
Q1: How are conflicts regarding operational budgets or resource allocation between departments resolved?
Answer: Operational budget allocations are established during annual planning cycles led by Finance. If mid-cycle resource conflicts arise between departments (e.g., Engineering requiring extra compute infrastructure vs. Logistics requesting additional hub equipment), the issue must be presented to the CFO and COO. If agreement cannot be reached, the CEO makes the final determination.
Q2: Which department holds authority to modify customer refund rules?
Answer: Customer Support owns the Customer Compensation Framework and Customer Dispute Resolution Policy. However, because refunds directly impact corporate financials, software workflows, and inventory tracking, any modification requires formal review by Finance, Product Management, and Engineering prior to implementation.
Q3: Who manages relationships and disputes with third-party logistics (3PL) partners?
Answer: Day-to-day management and operational delivery compliance belong to the Logistics department. Commercial contract negotiations, rate adjustments, and formal legal notices are managed jointly with Sales and Legal.
Q4: If an employee discovers a data vulnerability or security incident, who should they contact first?
Answer: Security maintains 24/7 oversight over system integrity. Employees must report suspected physical or digital security incidents immediately via the internal Incident Response Portal or by directly alerting the Security Operations Center (SOC).
Q5: Who owns the execution of last-mile delivery rider onboarding?
Answer: Logistics manages rider recruitment, route training, delivery app onboarding, and operational scheduling. HR provides secondary oversight regarding legal compliance, background check standards, and worker protection policies.
Q6: What is the process for proposing a structural change to a department or adding a new team?
Answer: Department heads must submit an Organizational Change Proposal to the Chief Human Resources Officer (CHRO) and the appropriate Executive Committee member. The proposal must detail business justification, headcount impact, budget implications, and updated reporting lines. Final approval rests with the CEO and CFO.
